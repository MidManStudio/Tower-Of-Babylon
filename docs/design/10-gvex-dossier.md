## 1. What G.V.E.X. is (yours)

**G.V.E.X.**, Gruesomely Volatile Extermination Xenoform, is the Niu Galactic Federation's designation for extreme, high-volatility extraterrestrial organisms captured across contested, ruined or quarantined worlds. Designation standard: `G.V.E.X.-[Threat Tier]-[Specimen ID]`. Authority: Niu High Command and the Tower of Babylon Containment Division.

The Federation does not destroy every anomalous lifeform it finds. Its containment apparatus harvests, catalogues and secures select specimens:

- **Collection and containment.** Specimens are extracted from lethal environments, given a threat tier (X0 to X6) and housed in reinforced containment vaults or in active sectors of the Tower.
- **Weaponisation and deployment.** When tactical pressure is wanted (breaking planetary resistance, culling populations, stocking the broadcast penal trials inside the Tower) contained specimens are deployed as living hazards.
- **Extermination.** A specimen judged unmanageable, obsolete or too expensive to keep is marked for total extermination, though containment protocols often fail against the upper tiers.

### Tier X0: baseline swarm and reconnaissance assets

**`X0-01` Ghouls.** Mindless necrotic-grade hostiles deployed in high numbers, in three mobility grades: slow, medium-velocity, and high-velocity runners. *Doctrine:* individually low-lethality; dangerous through gradual encirclement, numbers, and pinning targets into restricted sectors. *Notes:* standard low-tier crowd pressure across the lower sectors; more dangerous together with higher-tier assets.

**`X0-17` Clanckers.** Organic-hybrid mechanical constructs, living tissue on a hardened chassis. *Doctrine:* scan through solid walls and bulkheads to track subjects behind cover. *Notes:* they negate line-of-sight concealment, so subjects must keep moving and avoid static cover.

### Tier X2: sub-baseline olfactory hazard

**`X2-12` BuzzCunts.** High-mass wasp-like aerial organisms with a stinger, in a ranged acid-spit variant and a close-range dive variant. *Doctrine:* track targets strictly by scent; spit corrosive acid at range or dive at close quarters. *Notes:* odour suppression and scent-masking countermeasures directly reduce their acquisition range.

### Tier X3: mid-tier auditory and ambush assets

**`X3-14` Slenderen.** Tall, emaciated, high-mobility clawed humanoids with unstable, undulating tissue mass, in three variants: **bipedal** (upright high-speed pursuer), **quadrupedal / climber** (runs on all four limbs and scales vertical surfaces), **volatile / detonator** (closes the distance, shrieks, and detonates). *Doctrine:* hunt exclusively by sound and strike heavily by default; the detonators must be separated from or neutralised before they get close. *Notes:* acoustic discipline is mandatory in their sectors: gunfire, sprinting and heavy respiration accelerate engagement.

**`X3-06` Aracnid.** Multi-limbed arachnid xenoforms, two standard variants and one apex variant: **close-quarters climber** (walls, melee rushes), **ranged web-spinner** (immobilising webbing at distance), and the **apex hybrid** (melee and web, and it lays snare webs). *Doctrine:* track by sight and sound and change the terrain with webbing; a direct high-lumen beam blinds a standard specimen and breaks its visual tracking. *Notes:* light counters visual lock-on, at the risk of showing your position to other visual threats.

### Tier X4: high-tier colony, apex and brood assets

**`X4-04` Lumbricus Minor.** A dense aggregate of predatory worms that mass together and breach narrow fissures. *Doctrine:* track by sound and overwhelm by swarm mass; the cluster is neutralised by eliminating the **Prime Specimen (Queen)** that governs it. *Notes:* indiscriminate fire at the other segments wastes ammunition; go for the Prime Specimen.

**`X4-46` Lumbricus Major.** A single massive annelid for close-quarters combat; it leaves a high-viscosity trail that slows the target. *Doctrine:* tracks by sound and scent and cuts off escape with the slowing residue. **Structural rule:** the cranial/head segment must be destroyed; damaging the body segments makes the specimen **divide**. *Notes:* very dangerous up close, and body impacts multiply the active threats.

**`X4-44` MajourBuzzCunt.** An enlarged, high-threat evolution of the X2-12. *Doctrine:* ranged acid attacks, spawns subordinate BuzzCunts, and starts a **suicide detonation rush** when its structural integrity drops critical. *Notes:* keep your distance when its vitality nears the critical threshold, to avoid the point-blank detonation.

### Tier X5: severe deep-sector assets

**`X5-476` Troglobite.** Optically cloaked under the standard visual spectrum; seen only through thermal optics, or for a moment while it attacks. *Doctrine:* teleports short distances to stalk its target and strike from concealment, following acoustic signatures. *Notes:* thermal imaging is essential to find it before it strikes.

**`X5-886` Mandragora.** A botanical-venomous entity that anchors to vertical surfaces and moves across walls to evade countermeasures. *Doctrine:* a disorienting shriek on close approach (which draws nearby hostile assets), root-based strikes, and it captures and ingests targets within close range. *Notes:* a severe sector-control hazard; long proximity risks capture, ingestion and a secondary swarm converging.

### Tier X6: extreme unkillable asset

**`X6-999` InexorabilisMacholo.** The highest-tier X6 specimen, with no recorded neutralisation method. *Doctrine:* immune to standard and heavy neutralisation; it forces an immediate sector abandonment. *Notes:* engagement is strictly non-viable once it is deployed; subjects must leave the sector at once by **vertical transit** (the stairs).

## 5. Why this list needs an ECS (mine)

Read as data, every specimen is a row of the same few things: a **tier**, a **sense kit** (any of five senses with a range), a **body** (size, speed, health, an optional weak point), a **behaviour**, and **variants** of one species. Three things in it are not rows but **relations between creatures**: the Prime Specimen governs a swarm; the MajourBuzzCunt spawns subordinate BuzzCunts; a Lumbricus Major divides into more of itself when its body is hurt. And every counter is a **condition on the player** (light on you, noise you make, scent on you, being seen through thermal optics).

That is the shape of an entity-component-system: components (`Tier`, `Senses`, `Health`, `WeakPoint`, `Variant`), relationships (`governs`, `spawned_by`, `divided_from`), and systems that run over everything with the right components (sense, chase, attack, divide, detonate). Writing eleven creatures and their variants as eleven modules with file-scope state, as the three built ones are, makes the third creature as expensive as the first and does not give a server and a client two views of the same monsters in one process (`docs/gvex_session.md`). The decision is **Flecs** for the game; what it would take for mid-ecs to reach this use is written down in mid-engine's `docs/research-ecs-from-a-c-game.md`.

## 6. What the dossier leaves open (mine)

1. **No numbers.** Health, speed, sense ranges, damage and spawn counts are not in the dossier. They belong in a `monsters.mdix` (see `03`, "Procedural variation"), not in code.
2. **Mandragora's senses** are "pending field telemetry".
3. **X1** is empty; it may be deliberate.
4. **Which tiers on which floors.** `03` has a proposal (strides and arachnids on floors 1 to 5, and so on); the dossier's tiers (X0 to X6) suggest keeping the same idea with the new names. `11-babylon-01.md` describes the floor bands the dossier implies.
5. **Procedural variation.** `03` says the monsters are procedural, built from a body, a sense kit and a behaviour, and that "amalgamated" monsters combine two rows. The dossier has named specimens and does not mention amalgams; keep amalgams as the way to make more, or drop them?
