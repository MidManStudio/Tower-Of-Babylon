# Weapons, pickups and hands

## Overview

The player starts with nothing but hands and can punch. Weapons lie on the floor and are picked up: a pipe (melee, swings), a pistol, a pump-action shotgun and an assault rifle (guns: they fire projectiles and spend their own kind of ammo). The first person hands and whatever they hold are drawn in front of the camera and animated. The attack button does whatever the weapon in hand does. A SWAP button appears once the player owns more than one thing to hold.

Everything visible here is built in code from simple solid shapes ("parts"), so there are no model files to load and nothing to bundle. See "Hand models" below for why, and how to use a real model later.

## Modules (all in `src/weapons/`)

### `parts.h` and `parts.c`

A part is a tapered prism between two points: a limb, a finger, a box, a barrel. Four sides with a 45 degree twist make a box; six or eight make a cylinder. `Part_Cyl` and `Part_Box` build them. `Part_Draw` draws one through rlgl with a colour per vertex: a part with six or more sides is smooth shaded (each corner of the ring takes its light from its own outward normal, so a finger or the pipe reads as round), and a box or a 3 or 5 sided prism is shaded face by face. `Part_Blob(centre, axisEnd, radius, flat, upHint, colour)` makes a closed, smooth shaded ellipsoid (5 rings of 8 segments, 64 triangles; `Part_BlobLow` is 4 rings of 6, 36, used for fingers and knuckles) that is long toward `axisEnd` and `radius` across: it is what fists, knuckles and the rounded end of the pipe are made of. Each part is drawn in one `rlBegin(RL_TRIANGLES)` with all its vertices, not one begin per triangle. Both hands together are about 1250 triangles (a test keeps them under 1500). `PartList_Draw` draws a list and relies on depth testing to sort it; `PartList_DrawSorted` (far to near) is still there for use with depth testing off. `Parts_SetBrightness(b)` scales every colour, which is how the hands go dark in dark places. Winding of every triangle is chosen from the outward direction of its face, so a part is correct whichever way round it is built.

### `weapons.h` and `weapons.c`

The rules, with no drawing and no knowledge of enemies. The numbers are not in this file: the `WeaponDef` table is filled from `assets/config/weapons.mdix` by `src/config/game_config.c` (see `config.md`), and `Weapons_SetDefs` installs it. Until a config is loaded every row is zero, so anything that reads weapons loads the config first (`GameConfig_LoadBaked`, or `GameConfig_Init` in the game).

- **Weapons.** Hands: 10 damage, one punch per 0.45 s, reach 1.7 m, 50 degrees either side. Pipe: 35 damage, 0.80 s per swing, connects 0.31 s in (half of its 0.62 s animation, the bottom of the chop), reach 2.6 m, 60 degrees, knocks back 1 m. Pistol: 25 damage, 0.30 s between shots, pistol ammo, a tiny spread (0 to 1.5 degrees as it heats). Shotgun: 8 pellets of 11 damage in a 4.5 degree cone, 0.95 s between blasts, shells, loud. Rifle: 14 damage, 0.12 s between shots (8 a second), rifle ammo, spread 0.6 degrees cold and 5 degrees hot. The three guns' projectile numbers (speed, drop, range, falloff) are in `projectiles.mdix`.
- **Fire modes.** `FIRE_SEMI` one shot per press; `FIRE_AUTO` repeats while the trigger is held (the pistol and the rifle: the pistol keeps the hold-to-repeat it always had); `FIRE_PUMP` one shot per press, then the cooldown is the pump stroke (the shotgun). A press that comes within 0.15 s before the gun is ready is kept and fires when it is, so tapping on a phone is not punished for being a little early. Melee and punches repeat while held, as before. A SEMI or PUMP gun held down fires once.
- **Heat.** Each shot heats the gun by `heatPerShot` (1 is fully hot) and it cools by `heatRecover` a second; the spread of the next shot is `spreadDeg` when cold and `spreadMaxDeg` when hot (`Arsenal_SpreadDeg`). The rifle at full auto reaches its full spread in about 2 seconds and settles in 2 more once the trigger is let go. Heat only builds if a shot adds more than the cooldown takes away (`cooldown * heatRecover` below `heatPerShot`). Switching guns starts the new one cold.
- **Ammo.** Three kinds (`AmmoType`): pistol rounds, shells, rifle rounds. A gun names its kind and how many a pull costs. `Ammo_BoxAmount` is how many are in a floor box, taken from the gun that uses that ammo. `AMMO_MAX` is 99, a stack in the inventory.
- **Magazines and reloading.** A gun with `mag_size` above 0 fires from its magazine (`Arsenal.mag[weapon]`, kept per gun, so a gun put away keeps what is in it) and reloads from the reserve in the bag. The inventory only ever holds the reserve; rounds in the magazine are not in the bag. A gun with `mag_size = 0` fires straight from the bag, as every gun did before reloading existed (kept so a config can turn magazines off for one gun). Pistol 12 rounds, 1.4 s to reload; rifle 30 rounds, 2.0 s; shotgun 6 shells loaded one at a time, 0.55 s each (`reload_per_round = 1`).
  - `Arsenal_StartReload` (the R key, the RELOAD button) starts one. It refuses for anything but a gun with a magazine, a full magazine, an empty reserve, a reload already running, or a gun still being raised (under 60% up, the same test as attacking). `Arsenal_CanReload` is the same test without starting, and is what shows the RELOAD button.
  - A magazine reload moves `min(room in the magazine, reserve)` rounds when its `reload_secs` are up, and not before: the bag is charged only when the magazine is in. The trigger is ignored while it runs. A tube-fed gun adds one round every `reload_secs` until it is full or the reserve is empty, and pulling the trigger with at least one round in stops the reload and fires (the shotgun's usual trick); with none in, the trigger does nothing.
  - Pulling the trigger on an empty magazine clicks (`dryFire`) and starts the reload by itself, if there is reserve to load.
  - Taking another weapon in hand (swap, equip, drop, or a break) stops the reload; the magazine keeps its rounds. Starting again later starts from the beginning.
  - `Arsenal_Fill(weapon, rounds)` loads a gun that need not be in hand and returns how many went in (a pickup uses it); `Arsenal_TakeMag` empties a magazine and returns the rounds (a drop uses it); `Arsenal_Loaded` is what the gun in hand can fire before it needs a reload; `Arsenal_ReloadFraction` is 0 to 1 through the current step.
  - Events: `ArsenalEvents.reloadStart`, `reloadRound` (a round went in) and `reloadDone`.
- **Aiming down sights (ADS).** Each gun has four numbers in `weapons.mdix` (`ads_fov`, `ads_secs`, `ads_move`, `ads_spread`; the hands and the pipe have none and no sights): the field of view at full aim (the normal one is 60), the seconds to get there, the share of the walking speed left, and the share of the spread left. Pistol 52 degrees, 0.14 s, 0.80, 0.50; shotgun 54, 0.22, 0.65, 0.75; rifle 46, 0.20, 0.60, 0.50. All placeholders to feel out. They are read by the rules (`GameSim_Step`) and the player controller, not by the arsenal; what ADS does, when it is wanted and how the pose is built is in `player.md` and below. `GameSim_SpreadDeg` is the spread a shot fired now uses: `Arsenal_SpreadDeg` times the ADS share by how far in the player is. The loader (`game_config.c`) checks the ranges (field of view 20 to 60, time 0.02 to 2 s, move and spread shares above 0 and at most 1).
- **Durability.** A melee weapon with `durability` above 0 wears by one for every swing that connects (one swing is one wear however many enemies it hits; a swing at nothing costs nothing). The pipe has 30; the hands have 0 and never wear. `Arsenal.wear[weapon]` counts the swings; `Arsenal_Condition` is 1 for new down to 0, and 1 for anything that does not wear. `Arsenal_Wear` returns true on the swing that uses it up and resets the count; **the caller** (`BreakWeapon` in `game_sim.c`) then takes it out of the hand and out of the bag, so a broken pipe is gone, not merely unequipped. Wear belongs to the weapon's id, not to a particular item: there is one pipe, and a pipe dropped and picked up again keeps its wear (the old rule that picking a gun up always brings a clip applies likewise: see Known limits).
- `Arsenal` holds what is owned, the ammo of the weapon in hand (`ammo`: the RESERVE; the caller sets it each frame from the inventory and takes back what the arsenal used), the magazines, the wear, the reload state, what is in hand and the animation timers (cooldown, attack time, recoil, muzzle flash, an empty click, raising a weapon after switching, heat, the trigger's last state and the press buffer).
- `Arsenal_Update(dt, attackHeld, &events)` returns events and the caller acts on them: `shot` (the caller fires the projectiles: `Gunfire_Shoot`), `impact` (a melee blow connects now), `dryFire` (trigger pulled with nothing in the gun) and `reloadStart`, `reloadRound`, `reloadDone`. A new weapon cannot attack until it is 60% raised. An attack in progress carries on after the button is released.
- `Arsenal_Give` owns and equips a weapon (false if already owned), `Arsenal_Cycle` moves to the next owned weapon in the order hands, pipe, pistol, shotgun, rifle, `Arsenal_AddAmmo` adds rounds. `Arsenal_Equip` takes an owned weapon in hand (the inventory's EQUIP button) and `Arsenal_Remove` gives one up, putting the hands back if it was in hand (dropping). In the game the rounds live in the inventory (see `inventory.md`).
- `Aim_FromCamera` gives the camera's eye, forward, right and up; `Aim_MuzzleFor(aim, weapon)` is where a shot leaves that gun: 0.55 m ahead, 0.17 right and 0.11 below the eye for the pistol, 1.0 m ahead for the shotgun and 1.1 m for the rifle (where their barrels end), 0.15 right and 0.08 below. `Aim_Muzzle` is the pistol's.

### `gunfire.h` and `gunfire.c`

`Gunfire_Shoot(def, aim, map, spreadDeg, rng)` turns one pull of the trigger into projectiles, using only the weapon's row and its projectile config.

1. **Aim.** The shot is aimed at the point the crosshair is on: the first wall along the view ray, at least 2.5 m and at most 40 m away. It is not fired parallel to the view, so what is under the crosshair is what is hit even though the muzzle is to the side of and below the eye (a test fires at a wall 19 m away and the shot arrives on the view line, not 17 cm beside it).
2. **Start.** At the weapon's own muzzle, unless a wall lies between the eye and the muzzle (the player is pressed against it), in which case at just in front of the wall, so a shot cannot be fired through it. The projectile's first frame is then tested against that wall.
3. **Spread.** `Pattern_Directions` spreads the pellets around the straight line from the muzzle to the aim point, by the weapon's pattern, over `spreadDeg`. Each pellet gets its own speed in the config's range.
4. Each pellet is a projectile carrying the weapon's `damage`; distance takes it down (`projectiles.md`).

### `weapons_headless.c`

Build line in the file. **Aiming down sights** (each gun's four numbers are in range and the rifle zooms the most, the hands and pipe have none; at zero sway the gun slides exactly 0.13 / 0.15 across and 0.010 / 0.034 up, half way in is half way there, the hands go with it, the pipe ignores it, the aimed sway is under half the hip's, and `Viewmodel_Build` is the hip pose). The arsenal (hands, punches alternating, giving and equipping and removing, the pipe's swing, the pistol's ammo and rate), **magazines and reloading** (the three magazine sizes, a pickup is a full magazine, a manual reload, a short bag, the trigger ignored during a reload, an empty gun reloading itself and the bag paying for it, switching weapons cancelling it while the magazine keeps its rounds, the shotgun's shell by shell reload and its interruption, a gun with no magazine firing from the bag as before) and **melee durability** (wear on landed swings only, breaking resets the count, the hands never wear), the fire modes (a pump shotgun fires once however long it is held, ignores a tap during the pump, keeps a tap made just before it is ready, gives about a blast a second when tapped as fast as possible; a SEMI gun set up for the test fires once per press; a held rifle fires 8 a second, its spread has opened by a full second and nearly closed by three, and settles after; a pistol held down heats a little; 2 shells make 2 blasts), the aim frame, pickups (placement over 25 floors: open, unreserved, at least 10 steps of walking from the arrival point by the test's own flood fill, so also reachable, and 6 tiles apart; a new gun on floor 2 and floor 3, a gun already owned turning into its ammo, box sizes, which kinds turn up over 60 floors, every kind draws a model), melee against enemies, the view model (parts count, none wholly behind the camera, the long guns reach further than the pistol and the left hand is out on the foregrip, the pump moves more than 10 cm, a flash adds parts, under 1500 triangles with each long gun, the shotgun kicks harder than the pistol), the pipe's chop, the depth range, and shots at real enemies through the whole pipeline (gun, projectile, wall test, enemy): a rifle round 6 m away hits once, three pistol rounds kill a floor 1 enemy as before, a shotgun blast at 3 m lands most pellets and kills outright. With `VM_DUMP=/tmp/vm` it also writes the triangles of many poses (`/tmp/vm_<pose>.txt`), which were drawn offline (see below).

### `pickups.h` and `pickups.c`

The pool holds 24 pickups. A floor places 5 of them, the same way every time for the same floor (a small generator seeded from the floor's seed and number); the rest of the room is for dropped items. Candidate tiles are open, not reserved (the stair alcoves), at least 10 steps of walking from the arrival point (found by a flood fill, so every pickup is reachable), and at least 6 tiles from each other. Floor 1 has a pipe, a pistol, two boxes of pistol ammo and a medkit so everything can be tried. Floor 2 holds a shotgun for a player who has none and floor 3 a rifle; from then on a floor holds one weapon chosen at random from the pipe and the three guns. A weapon the player already owns is replaced by a box of its ammo (the pipe, which has none, by pistol ammo). From floor 2 on, one of the two ammo boxes is pistol ammo and the other a kind chosen among the guns that exist by then (no rifle rounds before floor 3). Every floor has a medkit, a stim shot (`PICKUP_STIM`, drawn as a small cyan syringe, labelled STIM SHOT; it becomes `ITEM_STIM` in the bag, `inventory.md`), a battery (floor 1 two; later floors a second one four times in ten) and, for a player who does not carry one, a flashlight (see `flashlight.md`). Each pickup carries a count (a box holds what its gun's `ammo_box` says: 8 pistol rounds, 6 shells, 24 rifle rounds; 1 for a weapon or medkit; or whatever was dropped) and a timer that stops a dropped ammo box being collected by walking for 2 s. `Pickups_Drop` puts a stack on an open tile 1.4 m (or 1.0, 0.6, 0) in front of the player. Ammo boxes are picked up by walking within 1.1 m (if they fit in the inventory); weapons and medkits need the USE button within 1.8 m. The medkit is a white case with a red cross; the shotgun and rifle are small models of themselves (barrel, receiver, pump and handguard, magazine); shells are a red box with a brass band and rifle rounds a drab one with a brass strip. Each is drawn as a bobbing, turning prop with a flat glowing ring on the floor and a thin beam up to 2.3 m so it can be seen across a dark room. Pickups come back when a floor is entered again, because floors are rebuilt, as enemies do.

### `viewmodel.h` and `viewmodel.c`

`Viewmodel_Build` makes the parts for the current pose; `Viewmodel_Draw` draws them. Parts are placed in the camera's frame (x right, y up, z forward) and moved into the world, so the hands follow the view, including the hit shake.

- A hand is a loose fist on a forearm that runs back to a fixed elbow off screen, so it stretches when the hand moves. The fist is rounded blobs: a back of the hand, a row of four knuckles, two joints per finger curling forward and down, and a thumb across the side; it is wider than the wrist, as a fist is. The sleeve is prison orange and ends well down the arm. Everything is smooth shaded, so it reads as a fist. Two earlier versions looked like shards: first four-sided fingers drawn in painter's order, then thin six-sided cylinders (which, seen from half a metre on a phone, were spikes and wedges). The hands sit 7 cm nearer the middle of the screen than before so they are not mostly under the jump and attack buttons.
- **Depth.** `Viewmodel_Draw` flushes the batch, clears the depth buffer (`ViewClip_ClearDepth`), sets a depth range of 0.04 to 4 for the hands (`ViewClip_Reapply`) and draws the parts unsorted with depth testing on, then puts the world range back. Before, the hands were drawn with depth testing off and sorted by the middle of each part, which is wrong for long overlapping parts (an arm against its fingers) and was the most visible glitch in the screenshots. A wall the player stands against still never hides the hands, because the depth buffer is clear when they are drawn.
- **Light.** `main.c` passes `Lighting_AtPoint(player position)`; hands are drawn at `0.22 + 0.78 * light` of their colour, so they go dim in a dead patch but never vanish. A muzzle flash lights them (at least 55% to 100% while it lasts).
- Idle sway, a walking bob that follows how fast the player moves, and the weapon rising from below for a moment after equip.
- Punch: the fist pulls back, snaps forward and across, and returns; the two fists alternate.
- **Pipe: a slanting overhead chop.** One swing is 0.62 s and the blow lands at half of it. 0 to 0.34 the arm rises and the pipe is cocked up and back over the right shoulder, leaning outward; 0.34 to 0.50 it comes down across the body, front tip leading, accelerating all the way (the strike is about 0.1 s); 0.50 to 0.62 it follows through low and to the left; then it recovers. The keyframes are `SLASH_*` at the top of the slash section in `viewmodel.c` (pitch, roll and yaw in radians, and a shift in metres), in the order rest, cocked, impact, follow-through. The hand and the pipe both turn about the middle of the grip, and the pipe takes the whole turn while the fist takes about half, so the grip never slides in the fist. The previous pose spun the pipe about the chest in a flat arc; the player asked for a vertical, angled downward slash like a baton.
- **Slash trail.** While the pipe is moving fastest (0.36 to 0.66 of the swing) the pipe's tip and middle are recorded each frame in camera space and drawn as a fading ribbon (0.2 s) with no depth write, bright at the tip and gone toward the grip, so the arc of the blow shows.
- Pistol: held in the right hand and cupped by the left. A shot kicks it back and up, and a muzzle flash shows for about 0.05 s.
- **Aimed down the sights** (`Viewmodel_BuildAimed(..., ads01)`, `Viewmodel_DrawAimed`; `Viewmodel_Build` and `Viewmodel_Draw` are the hip pose of the same functions). `ads01` is the player's eased aim amount. For a gun only (the hands and the pipe ignore it) the gun and both hands slide together to put the sights on the middle of the view: the pistol 0.13 to the left and 0.010 up, the shotgun and rifle 0.15 and 0.034 (`ADS_SLIDE_*`, the difference being the 2 cm and 3 cm the long guns sit to the right and lower), the idle sway and walking bob fall to 30% (`ADS_SWAY_LEFT`). The left hand is the right one mirrored, so its x runs the other way (a bug the test caught). The sleeves stay anchored at the screen's edge, so the hands move a little less than the gun. The hands are drawn with the world's projection, so a zoom also enlarges them somewhat. **Strafe tilt (round 6, the user: "when aiming and moving left or right the gun tilts a bit in the direction of the move"):** `Viewmodel_BuildAimedSway(..., ads01, strafe01)` (and `Viewmodel_DrawAimedSway`; `BuildAimed`/`DrawAimed` are the same with 0) turns the whole picture, the gun, both hands, sleeves and the muzzle flash, about the view's centre line by `Viewmodel_StrafeTilt = -strafe01 * ADS_STRAFE_TILT (0.08 rad, 4.6 degrees) * ads01`, so moving right leans the top of the gun to the right, and the sights, which are at the centre at full aim, stay where they are (a roll about the centre line moves a point on it by nothing). It is the tilt at full aim and a fraction of it on the way in, none at the hip, none for the hands and the pipe (no sights). The cross-section hints of boxes and flattened blobs are turned too (`UpHint`), or a rolled gun would have square-on edges. `strafe01` is `PlayerStrafe01` (`player.md`), eased by `Viewmodel_StrafeSwayStep(current, target, dt)` over `VIEWMODEL_STRAFE_EASE_SECS` (0.1 s, exponential, the same at any frame rate), which `main.c` calls every frame with the frame time (a static in the drawing block). Tests in `weapons_headless.c` (about 40 checks): the sign and size of the tilt, the clamp, every gun's parts turning rigidly (each point's x and y by the roll, z untouched), the top leaning toward the move and the bottom the other way, left mirrored, the box hints turning, nothing at the hip, half the tilt at half aim, no change for the pipe, `BuildAimed` unchanged, the easing's steady climb without overshoot and its frame-rate independence; 5 mutations caught (hint not turned, sign flipped, no aim weight, the easing not frame-rate independent, the roll's mixing dropped). **Never seen on a screen:** whether 4.6 degrees is "a bit" (`ADS_STRAFE_TILT` is the knob; at a typical aimed strafe the share is 1, so this is the whole tilt), whether 0.1 s feels right, and a gun slightly mis-lit at an angle. Never seen on a screen: the offsets come from the sight positions in the part tables (the rear sight of the rifle is at about 0.0 after `Rest()`; the front sights are within a centimetre of the middle line at full ADS), not from a render.
- **Shotgun and rifle** are two-handed. The right hand holds the grip behind the receiver; the left hand moves out to the foregrip (the shotgun's pump, the rifle's handguard), under the barrel, about 0.6 m ahead of the right. Both guns sit 2 cm to the right and 3 cm lower than the pistol so the barrel stays out of the crosshair. There is no stock in view: it is tucked into the shoulder behind the camera, as in most first person games (a first version drew one and it filled the corner of the screen, 12 cm from the lens). The shotgun is a steel barrel with a tube under it, a boxy receiver, a wooden grip and a wooden pump; during the pump stroke (from 25% to 85% of the 0.6 s after a blast) the pump and the left hand slide 15 cm back and forward. The rifle is a polymer body with a receiver, a handguard, a barrel with a muzzle brake, a magazine, a front and a rear sight. Recoil scales with the gun's `kick` (`kick ^ 0.7`, so the pistol is unchanged at 1.0): the shotgun kicks back about 10 cm and tilts 14 degrees, the rifle a few centimetres and 5 degrees; a long gun's receiver is close to the lens, so it uses 0.06 m and 0.13 rad where the pistol uses 0.07 m and 0.20 rad per unit of kick. The muzzle flash is at each gun's own muzzle and the shotgun's is 1.7 times the pistol's. Both hands and a long gun are about 1420 triangles (the limit the test keeps is 1500).

I could not see the game running, so the poses were checked by drawing the same triangles offline from the camera's point of view with a depth buffer and near-plane clipping (idle, both punches, the pipe at rest and at eleven points through a swing, the pistol idle and firing, and the shotgun and rifle idle, firing, mid-pump and equipping, each also from the side and from above to check where the left hand is). Clipping against the near plane matters: a first version of the offline picture skipped any triangle that crossed it, which made a ring at the end of the left sleeve that is not in the game. They read as hands and as the right weapons, and the chop reads as a stroke from upper right, down across the body, to forward and low. How they feel and how big they look on the phone is not verified.

### Melee against enemies

`EnemiesMeleeHit` and `Spiders_MeleeHit` are described in `docs/enemies.md`. `GameSim_Step` calls them when the arsenal reports an `impact`, aiming along the floor-plane part of the look direction from the camera.

### Wiring (`GameSim_Step` in `src/sim/game_sim.c`; the buttons and the HUD in `main.c`)

- The attack button (`F` on a keyboard) is held to attack. Its label is PUNCH, SWING or FIRE for the weapon in hand. A gun that needs a fresh press per shot (the shotgun) must be tapped.
- SWAP (`Q` on a keyboard) appears when more than one thing can be held. USE (`E`) now also picks up a weapon within reach; it shows "PICK UP" and the name (PIPE, PISTOL, SHOTGUN, RIFLE, MEDKIT). USE and SWAP act once per press, not on every frame they are held.
- The weapon in hand is always on screen, bottom centre, right-aligned at x = 620 (`WeaponReadout` in `hud.c`): a gun shows the rounds loaded, large, and the reserve after a slash ("12 / 36"; orange at a quarter of the magazine, red when empty, a blinking RELOAD or NO AMMO hint when the magazine is empty), and a progress bar with RELOADING while it reloads; a pipe shows a condition bar that goes green, orange, red, with FALLING APART blinking under a quarter. The hands show nothing. The STATS popup shows the same numbers (`12 / 36`, or `18 swings left`). A message ("PICKED UP: PIPE", "+8 AMMO") shows above the health bar for about 1.8 s.
- RELOAD: the `R` key, or a round button at (668, 408), radius 30, under SLIDE (not in the sketch: it is the one free spot big enough, 13 px clear of SLIDE and 7 px of the fire pad; move it in `touch_controls.c`). It exists only while a reload can start (`Arsenal_CanReload`), is grey, maroon when the magazine is empty, and while a reload runs it turns amber with a ring filling round it and cannot be pressed. A press that cannot reload says why when that helps: "MAGAZINE FULL" or "NO SPARE AMMO" (silent for the hands, the pipe, or a reload already running). It does nothing with the inventory open or when dead.
- A shot calls `Gunfire_Shoot` with the spread the gun had before the shot heated it. Projectiles are moved and tested against the walls and enemies each frame (`ResolveProjectileHits(map, HitAnything)`: the monsters first, then the swarm), and drawn after everything else in the 3D pass. Hits show the same hit marker as before.
- Noise: a shot is heard by enemies out to the gun's `noise_metres` for its `noise_secs` (pistol 28 m for 1.5 s as before, shotgun 40 m for 2.2 s, rifle 34 m for 1.2 s, which a rifle at full auto keeps renewing).
- A gun comes with a clip of its own ammo (`pickup_ammo`: pistol 12, shotgun 6, rifle 30). The first `mag_size` rounds go into the gun's magazine and the rest into the bag (all of it into the bag for a gun with no magazine); what does not fit in the bag is dropped as a box beside the player. With the present numbers a picked-up gun is simply full and the bag is untouched. Dropping a gun empties its magazine back into the bag (overflow is left on the floor like a pickup's).
- Sounds (`audio_events.c`): there is no reload recording yet; `AudioEvents_Reload` plays the flashlight's click pitched down for the magazine out, pitched up for it in, the UI click for a shell, and the pump after the last shell of a tube-fed gun. `AudioEvents_Break` is two low pipe hits. Both are placeholders in one place, to be replaced by recipes of their own.
- Viewmodel: while reloading the gun drops 7 cm, tips up and the off hand goes down and back (more for a two-handed gun), and a tube-fed gun's hand bobs once per shell. `Arsenal.reloadBlend` eases the pose in and out over about a fifth of a second so it never pops. Written blind: not seen on a screen.
- The diagnostics overlay has a `config:` line (see `config.md`).
- Pickups and the arsenal persist through death. Pickups are respawned with the floor.

## Hand models: what was found, and why parts were used

Searched for ready-made first person hands:

- OpenGameArt, "Low Poly FPS Rifle and Hands" (CC0, glTF, OBJ and FBX, with a shooting animation). This is the best fit for raylib. The hands are posed on a rifle, so they would suit a rifle viewmodel, not an empty-handed pose.
- itch.io, "PSX FPS Hands" by NiKo_Gillsky (CC0). Delivered as FBX, which raylib cannot load.
- itch.io, FUZE "Hands 3D Pack" (.glb). Its licence restricts passing the files on, which makes it unsuitable for a repository.

None of these could be downloaded from where the game is built, and without a way to try them in the real WASM build a model loader would have been untested code. The parts version loads nothing, costs no download, matches the flat low poly look of the enemies and is fully animated today. To use a real model later: put the glTF or OBJ under `assets/` (mray bundles that folder), load it with `LoadModel`, and draw it in `Viewmodel_Draw` at the same camera-relative position in place of the part list. The arsenal, pickups and melee code do not change.

## Fixes and Problems

- Everything above is covered by `weapons_headless.c`, `projectiles_headless.c` and `config_headless.c` except how it looks and feels on the phone.
- The first rifle numbers could never heat: at full auto it cooled 0.144 a shot and heated 0.12. Found while writing the heat test; the rifle's `heat_recover` is now 0.5 and the pistol's 1.0, and the rule is written in `weapons.mdix`.
- A first version of the long-gun models drew a stock. It was 12 cm from the lens and filled the corner of the view, and a heavy shotgun recoil rotated the receiver into the lens. Both were found by drawing the poses offline, not by a test.
- A full stack of one kind of ammo used to stop every other box nearby being picked up (the pickup loop stopped at the first one that did not fit); now it skips that box and takes the others.
- Spiders on the ceiling cannot be reached with melee (they are out of reach vertically), only with the pistol.
- Pickup details stand at least 1.5 cm clear of what they sit on (the medkit's cross, the ammo box's strip, the pipe pickup's grip and band), because surfaces a few millimetres apart flicker against each other on the phone at any distance.
- Pickup props are drawn in the world; the view model after a depth clear (see above).
- The pipe's tip is behind the forearm for part of the follow-through, as it points away from the camera. The trail shows where it went.

## Tuning

All the numbers for weapons are in `assets/config/weapons.mdix` (and the shots they fire in `projectiles.mdix`), each described by the comments at the top of the file; there is no longer a table in `weapons.c`. `AMMO_MAX` (99) is in `weapons.h`; the muzzle positions are `k_muzzle` in `weapons.c`; the press buffer is `PRESS_BUFFER_SECS`. `PICKUP_REACH`, `AMMO_AUTO_REACH` and `MAX_PICKUPS` are in `pickups.h`. Hand size and position: `HAND_SCALE`, `WRIST` and `REST_SHIFT` at the top of `viewmodel.c`; poses are in `Viewmodel_Build`. The slash: the `SLASH_*` keys and the five phase boundaries in `SlashPose`; the trail: `TRAIL_LIFE` and the window in `Viewmodel_Draw`. Hand brightness range: the `0.22 + 0.78 * b` in `Viewmodel_Draw`. Magazine sizes, reload times and the pipe's durability are `mag_size`, `reload_secs`, `reload_per_round` and `durability` in `weapons.mdix`; the 60% raise before a reload may start is `equipT >= 0.6` in `Arsenal_CanReload`; the reload pose is the `reloadBlend` block in `Viewmodel_Build`.

## Known limits

- Four weapons so far and three kinds of ammo. The pistol is set to hold-to-repeat as it always was (`fire_mode = FireMode.AUTO`); change it to `SEMI` in `weapons.mdix` for one shot per tap.
- The pipe's blow hits horizontally in front of the player (`EnemiesMeleeHit` is a floor-plane arc); the chop is vertical to look at but the hit test is unchanged.
- No sound yet: no punch, swing, shot, click or pickup sounds.
- A gun picked up always brings its clip, as it did before magazines, so dropping and picking the same gun up again gives a fresh clip (the dropped gun's rounds go back to the bag, so nothing is lost, but a gun on the floor does not remember what was in it). Fixing it means a pickup carrying the rounds in its count, which the netcode chat should see first.
- Durability belongs to the weapon id, not to the item: a pipe dropped and picked up keeps its wear, and there is only one kind of pipe. A second pipe would need the wear carried by the item.
- When the pipe breaks it is removed outright (no "broken pipe" item to repair or scrap); that is a design choice to confirm.
- The reload sounds are borrowed from other effects and the reload pose was never seen on a screen.
- The hands have no individual finger animation; they are fixed fists.

## `tools/viewmodel_raster.py`

An offline picture of the first person poses, because the sandbox the game is written in has no GPU: a small z-buffer rasteriser (numpy and Pillow) that reads the triangle dumps `weapons_headless` writes with `VM_DUMP`, and draws them from the camera at the origin looking down -z with a 60 degree field of view at 800 by 450 (as on the phone), or from the side or from above. Triangles are clipped against the near plane (0.04 m, the hands' range) so an arm that starts behind the camera is not drawn with holes. It shows geometry, proportions and where the hands are; it cannot show GPU behaviour (the half precision bug in `phone-rendering.md` was invisible in it).

### Two weapons equipped at most
`ARSENAL_MAX_EQUIPPED` is 2 (the hands do not count). In the arsenal `owned[]` now means *equipped*; a weapon the player only carries is an item in the inventory bag, which can hold more. `Arsenal_Give` equips and takes in hand when a place is free and returns false when both are taken; `Arsenal_Replace(in, out)` swaps an equipped weapon for one from the bag and takes it in hand; `Arsenal_IsEquipped` and `Arsenal_EquippedCount` ask; `Arsenal_Remove` puts an equipped weapon away (the hands again if it was in hand; the item stays in the bag unless dropped). Cycling (SWAP) goes round the hands and the equipped weapons only. Tested in `tests/weapons_headless.c` ("Two weapons equipped at most"). Not seen on a screen.
