# input

## Overview

Everything the player does with their hands comes through `src/input/`. Fingers, mouse and keyboard go in; one `InputFrame` of intentions comes out (move, look, sprint, jump, fire, use, swap, light, slide, reload, dive), so `main.c` never asks raylib which key is down and never knows which platform it is on. The controls follow the Call of Duty Mobile arrangement:

- **Left half: a floating move stick.** It appears where the thumb lands, anywhere in the left half (kept far enough in that the whole ring is on screen), and stays put while the finger moves. Forward is up the screen. It is quick: a 4% dead zone, and full walking speed at half the ring's radius (`stickFullAt` 0.50, about 43 px), so a short drag already moves you (a 15 px drag is about 29% of full speed, 30 px about two thirds). The rest of the ring is the run-up to the sprint.
- **Sprint is not a button, and it works in every direction (omni sprint).** An icon sits above the stick. Push the knob all the way out, any way round the ring, and the icon turns yellow: you are sprinting. A ring around the icon fills as the knob nears the rim, so you can see how far to go. Once started it holds until the knob comes back under 80% (so it does not flicker at the edge); swinging round the rim does not end it. How fast each direction goes (forward 1, left and right slower, backwards slowest) is the player controller's (`player.md`), not the stick's. Orange means the push is there but the player is too tired.
- **Right side: a fixed fire pad that also aims, by dragging.** The pad does not float. The finger on the pad fires, and moving that finger turns the camera by exactly how far it moves (`fireLookGain` 1.0), as the first version did; the disc stays where it is. While it is held with a gun in hand the player also aims down the sights (`player.md`). This is `TC_AIM_DRAG`, the default: the user tried a joystick version of the pad (`TC_AIM_JOYSTICK`: the knob follows the finger and the camera turns at a rate set by how far out it is, `aimRatePx` 720 px/s at full deflection) and said it was hard to aim and turned all the way round, and to put the old one back. The joystick code is kept behind `TcContext.aimMode`, since "drag or joystick" is a natural option for the settings screen that is to come; until then `main.c` passes `TC_DEFAULT_AIM_MODE` every frame. The enum has drag as 0, so a context that says nothing gets the old feel. A finger on a free spot of the right half is the look finger (it turns the camera by how far it moves, times `lookGain` 2.0, so a short swipe turns well; the fire finger's gain is 1.0, so the two are different on purpose, and either is one number in `TcLayout`). JUMP is a button; USE, SWAP, LIGHT, SLIDE and RELOAD appear only when they have something to act on (RELOAD: when the gun in hand has room in its magazine and rounds in the bag).
- **A DIVE button** (r 37, in SLIDE's place at 606, 357) appears only when `diveAvailable`: in the air after a jump that began from a sprint (`GameSim_DiveButtonShown`). One tap, on the frame the finger lands; the game reads the stick at that moment for the direction, so keep the stick finger where the dive should go. SLIDE and DIVE are never up together (a slide needs a forward sprint on the ground). There are no lean buttons: leaning is automatic, at wall corners (`player.md`).
- **STATS** (top left) opens the stats popup (`docs/hud.md`); INV opens the inventory.

A finger keeps the job it had when it went down until it lifts: a look finger that slides over JUMP does not jump, a camera finger that slides onto the fire pad does not fire, a stick finger that wanders into the right half is still the stick.

Keyboard (web and desktop): W A S D, Shift sprint (any direction), Space jump, F fire (and, with a gun, aim down the sights while held), E use, Q swap, R reload, V dive (in the air after a sprinting jump), C or Left Ctrl slide (forward only), L light, I or Tab inventory, P stats. A mouse button held down counts as one more finger, so a PC browser plays like a phone. On the desktop build the mouse is locked to the window and turns the camera with no button held, and the left button fires.

## Which controls exist where (`docs/platforms.md`)

| Capability | Web | Android | Desktop |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TOWER_INPUT_TOUCH` (touch logic compiled, controls drawn) | yes | yes | no |
| `TOWER_INPUT_KEYBOARD` (keys read) | yes | no | yes |
| `TOWER_INPUT_POINTER_LOCK` (mouse look with no button) | no | no | yes |

The browser may be on a phone or a PC, so it decides by use: the touch controls are drawn until a key is pressed, are hidden while the keyboard is in use, and come back with the next real finger. A mouse never switches the scheme.

## Modules

### `ui_rect.h`
A rectangle and a circle in canvas pixels (the canvas is 800 x 450) with `Contains` and `Overlaps` (circle with circle, and circle with rectangle, for a layout made of round buttons). No raylib, so the touch logic, the HUD layout and their tests need only a C compiler.

### `touch_controls.h` and `touch_controls.c`
**What it does:** the touch controls as a state machine with no raylib in it. `Tc_Update(state, fingers, count, context, out)` runs once a frame. A finger that lands is given a job by where it lands and what the game says is available (a tap-only region, the stick, one of the buttons, the fire pad, the look finger, or nothing) and keeps it. The output: move vector (dead zone 0.04, rescaled so the edge of the dead zone is 0 and `stickFullAt` is 1), look movement in canvas pixels (the camera finger times `lookGain`; the fire finger in `TC_AIM_DRAG` times `fireLookGain`; with the joystick aim: the deflection times `aimRatePx` times the frame's `dt`), the pad's deflection (`aimX`, `aimY`, joystick only), sprint, fire/jump/use/swap held, light, slide, reload and dive as one-frame presses, and the numbers the HUD draws (stick anchor and knob, the fire pad's knob, how close the knob is to a sprint, whether a camera finger is down). The layout is one `TcLayout` shared with the HUD.

**The layout (a placeholder, from the owner's sketch).** The HUD is to become customisable later, so these positions are not a design to protect: they are the one table `g_layout` in `touch_controls.c`, measured off a hand-drawn sketch (1920 x 1080, scaled by 5/12 to the canvas). Every control is a circle: the move stick's ring r 87 with an inner disc r 56 (idle at 128, 326), the sprint icon r 22 above it, PICKUP (the USE button) r 46 at 639, 172, JUMP r 37 at 760, 175 (above the aim joystick, out at the right edge), the fire pad r 71 at 724, 315 with a button disc r 42 and a bullet in it, SLIDE r 37 at 606, 357, RELOAD r 30 at 668, 408 (not in the sketch: the one free spot big enough, under SLIDE, 13 px clear of it and 7 px clear of the fire pad; the layout test checks it). The sketch has no SWAP, LIGHT or INV button, so they went to free spots: SWAP (r 30) at 632, 258 between PICKUP and the pad, LIGHT (r 32) at 700, 88 above them, INV (`inventory_ui.c`) moved to the top right corner, mirroring STATS. A small stray dot in the sketch near the top middle was ignored. The stick is bigger than before (ring 87, not 50), but full speed is now at half of it, so the thumb does not travel far; sprint still starts at 0.92 of the ring. DIVE is not in the sketch either: it takes SLIDE's place (the two are never shown together), a placeholder like the rest.

**The context** is how the game gates the controls, every frame: `locked` (a door is opening or the player is dead: everything zero, but the stick is still drawn and carries on when it unlocks), `walkOnly` (the inventory is open: only the stick works, the right half is for taps, and a camera finger that is down lets go at once and stays let go until it lifts), `useAvailable`, `swapAvailable`, `lightAvailable`, `slideAvailable`, `reloadAvailable`, `diveAvailable` (a button that is not available is not there: a finger on its spot is the camera), `dt` (seconds since the last frame, for the joystick aim; 0 turns nothing), `aimMode` (0 = drag), and `blocked` (regions that only take taps: STATS, INV, the open popup, the diagnostic rows; a finger there does not become the stick or the camera, the tap is handled by the HUD).

**Decisions:** the fire finger aims because that is how one thumb fires and aims at once, and because the old split (a fire button, then lift and reach for the camera) is what makes a shooter hard on a phone. The stick's anchor does not follow the finger (that is the Call of Duty Mobile behaviour; a following stick drifts under the thumb). Sprint needs only the rim: it used to need a direction within about 70 degrees of straight ahead, because running backwards at the full 7.6 m/s through the rim was never what the thumb meant. With omni movement the direction no longer decides whether you sprint, only how fast (`PLAYER_STRAFE_MUL`, `PLAYER_BACKPEDAL_MUL`). The stick was reported as insensitive (a drag of about 15 px, 17% of the ring, gave about 10% of full speed, because full speed was at the rim and the dead zone was 7 px) and turning as slow (a full turn took about 1250 canvas px of swiping): the stick now reaches full speed at half the ring, and touch look has a gain of 2 (it is applied in the touch logic, so the mouse and the shared `PlayerApplyLook` are unchanged).

### `input.h` and `input.c`
**What it does:** the one place raylib is asked about the player. Collects the fingers (and the mouse as a finger), runs the touch logic, reads the keyboard and the locked mouse where the platform has them (each behind its capability, so the code for a device a platform lacks is not compiled there), applies the same `locked` and `walkOnly` rules to the keyboard and mouse, turns USE and SWAP into once-per-press (and reports `useHeld` too: the unlock console is worked by holding USE, so the keyboard's E is now read as held, not as a press), and reports `gesture` (a finger, click or key this frame: a browser opens the sound card only after one). `Input_NewTap` returns a finger that went down this frame, for the buttons that are taps (STATS, INV, the inventory screen).

**Menus:** `Input_Menu` (a step left or right, a confirm, whether anything touched the device) is what the character selection screen reads (`characters.md`); its keyboard reads are behind `TOWER_INPUT_KEYBOARD` like the rest, and it does not run the touch logic, so a finger on a menu never becomes a stick.

**Decisions:** it takes no game types, only the `InputContext` (the same struct the touch logic uses), so it can be tested and reused. The inventory and stats toggles from the keyboard are returned as flags; the buttons for them are taps handled where the screens are.

### `tests/input_headless.c`
**What it covers (122 checks):** the floating stick (appears under the thumb, stays put, forward is -y, full speed at `stickFullAt`, dead zone, clamped near the edges, no stick above the zone or after 400 px), that a short drag moves you (15 px is over a quarter speed, 30 px most of the way, a diagonal 6 px drag already moves), sprint (the ring fills, starts at 0.92 in any direction, holds to 0.80, swinging round the rim keeps it, ends when the knob comes in or the finger lifts), roles (second left finger ignored, the camera finger turns by how far it moves times the gain, does not press JUMP in passing, a second camera finger is ignored), the fixed fire pad as a drag, the default (a context that says nothing gets it, one to one with the finger, the disc stays put, nothing turns when held still, a camera finger sliding on does not fire, an occupied pad ignores a second finger, locked is zero) and as a joystick when asked (the knob follows the finger and stops on the ring, the response curve, the rate times `dt`, it keeps turning while held still, a dead zone, no frame time means no turn), the buttons (only when available, light, slide, reload and DIVE once per tap, the stick finger staying on the stick through a DIVE tap, DIVE sitting where SLIDE does and winning if both were ever up, on the ground it is the camera; no reload button when there is nothing to load or only walking is allowed), tap-only regions, `walkOnly` and `locked`, twelve fingers at once, and the layout (nothing outside the screen; no two controls overlapping, tested as circles and rectangles, not bounding squares; thumb-sized targets; the popup clear of every control; and the layout matching the sketch within 2 px, a block to delete when the HUD becomes customisable). Every pixel offset in the tests is a fraction of the stick radius, so retuning the layout does not break them. Five deliberate breakages of the logic were each caught, and so was moving JUMP onto the pad.

## Not verified

- Nothing here has been run on a phone. The logic is tested without a screen; the drawing, the thumb reach on a real 6.6 inch screen, the sizes of the targets and how the fire pad feels (the drag has the old gain again, `fireLookGain` 1.0; the joystick numbers `aimRatePx`, `aimDeadzone`, `aimFullAt`, `aimCurve`, and `lookGain` and `stickFullAt` are first guesses for the feel), and whether the DIVE button, in SLIDE's place, is reachable in the air, need a person with the device. Positions and sizes are in `TcLayout` and `hud_layout.c`, one place each.
- The desktop path (`TOWER_INPUT_POINTER_LOCK`, the keyboard-only scheme) compiles and is checked by `tests/platform_check.sh`, but no desktop build exists to run it.
- A browser on a phone with a hardware keyboard attached would flip to the keyboard scheme on a key press and hide the touch controls until the next touch. Intended; not seen.

## Fixes and Problems

- The move stick felt dead (a drag of 15 px gave about 10% speed: the dead zone was 8% of the ring, 7 px, and full speed was at the rim, 87 px) and touch turning felt slow. Full speed is now at half the ring with a 4% dead zone, touch look has a gain of 2, and the fire pad is a fixed joystick whose knob follows the finger. Sprint is omni. These numbers are guesses to be tuned on a phone; they are all in `TcLayout`.
- The layout was redrawn from the owner's sketch (round buttons, a larger stick, JUMP above the aim joystick, PICKUP, SLIDE and the pad placed as drawn). The tests were rewritten in fractions of the stick radius and the layout checks now use real circle geometry. A round button has no corners: a finger on the corner of its bounding square is the camera.
- Replaces the touch code that was written inline in `main.c` (a fixed stick, a RUN toggle button with an idle timer, a shoot rectangle that fired but did not aim). The old behaviour that was kept: a finger's job is fixed when it lands, walking works with the inventory open, a locked game ignores everything.
