# Two players and the G.V.E.X. in one session: the plan

**Decided (2026-11-25):** monsters come into sessions, and they are the G.V.E.X. (`design/10-gvex-dossier.md`); the game uses an ECS for them, **Flecs** (chosen earlier; mid-ecs cannot do this use yet, see mid-engine's `docs/research-ecs-from-a-c-game.md`). This file is the plan from what the code was to "two players on two phones fighting the same creatures", and says what is built. **Built and tested: slice S1** (the same ghouls on both screens). Not built: S2 to S5, and what the weapons need (the end of this file).

## Where things stand (checked in the code)

- The server runs **only the movement** of players (`NetSim_Step`, `net_move_sim.c`). It does not run `GameSim`; the snapshot is the owner's movement state plus the other players' position and yaw (`NetSnapshot`: 77 floats-and-bytes for the owner, 18 bytes per other player).
- `GameSim` has **one player**, and its monsters, projectiles, pickups, props and stair clock keep their state **at file scope** in their own modules: one simulation per process (`sim.md`, "Not verified" and "For multiplayer"). The host of a session runs the server and its own client in **one process**, so the server's monsters and the client's picture of them cannot both live in those globals.
- Nothing replicates a monster, a shot, a hit or a health value. Sessions have no combat at all; `net_shots.md` step 1 (view lag, pose history, the shot seed) is the only part of combat that exists.

## What an ECS buys here, in one sentence

Flecs worlds are **instances**: a server's world of creatures and a client's world of what the server said can live side by side in one process, which file-scope modules cannot do. A spike confirmed it on this machine: two `ecs_init()` worlds in one program, a system running in one and not touching the other.

**The spike (not in the repo):** the amalgamated `flecs.c` (4 MB) and `flecs.h` (1.2 MB) from the Flecs repository, built as a custom build with `FLECS_CUSTOM_BUILD` and only `FLECS_SYSTEM`, `FLECS_PIPELINE`, `FLECS_TIMER`, `FLECS_MODULE`, `FLECS_PARSER`, `FLECS_QUERY_DSL`, `FLECS_LOG` and `FLECS_OS_API_IMPL`, with `gcc -std=gnu99 -O2`: 10 seconds on one core, a 1.4 MB object. A first run without `FLECS_QUERY_DSL` aborted silently: a system declared with a signature string needs the query DSL addon.

## The path, in slices (each ends in something two phones can check)

**S1: the same ghouls on both screens (built, round 9).**
- Flecs is vendored (`third_party/flecs/`, below). `src/gvex/gvex_world.c` owns **one Flecs world per game**: components `GvPos`, `GvYaw`, `GvBody`, `GvHealth`, `GvIdent`, `GvSenses`, `GvMove`, `GvState`, `GvTarget`, two systems in the update phase (`GhoulSense`, then `GhoulMove`), a query that reads the creatures out in id order, `GvexWorld_Damage` (a creature at 0 health is deleted).
- The Ghoul (`X0-01`) follows the single-player cylinder enemies' rules, over every player at once: it **sees** a player within 14 m with a clear line, **hears** one whose noise (`PlayerNoise`) reaches it, even through walls, **stays** after what it sensed for 3 s, **chases** the nearest player it senses (or, while it is staying, the nearest), **attacks** within 1.8 m (no damage yet), holds the attack until the player is 2.43 m away (1.35 reaches) and closes up to its reach while it does, and walks a wall at a time so it slides along walls. The three **mobility grades** of the dossier come from the creature's hash (slow, medium, runner) and scale the single-player chase speed by 0.75, 1.0 and 1.25: **placeholders**, the dossier gives no numbers (`Gvex_GradeSpeedFactor`; yours to tune, they will move into `monsters.mdix`).
- The spawns are `EnemyPlan_Spawns` (`src/enemies/enemy_plan.h`, header-only), which `enemy_manager.c` now uses too: **one copy of where cylinders spawn, what they look like, where they can stand and what they can see**, so a session and the solo game cannot drift (the single-player tests pass unchanged).
- Snapshots carry them (protocol 5, `net.md`): 19 bytes a creature, 32 at most, 754 bytes for a full snapshot. The server (`NetServer`) steps the world every tick with the started players as targets; the client keeps what the server said in its snapshot ring and interpolates it (`NetClient_MonsterViews`). **The client's view is plain arrays, not a Flecs world** (the plan said a view world): a view has no systems to run, so an ECS would only be a second place to keep the same numbers. Where the ECS earns its keep is the server.
- `PlayUI_Draw3D` draws them with `EnemyLook` from the floor map's seed and the id, lit like the other players.
- **Limits of S1:** the floor only (no stairs: the stair modules are process-global too, `sim.md`), no damage to players, no way to hurt a creature except `GvexWorld_Damage` from code, one kind. A host with `TOWER_GVEX` 0 sends no creatures and the format is the same.
- Tests: `tests/gvex_check.sh` (`testing.md`): 72 checks under the sanitizers (38 for the world, 34 for the wire and the two screens), the earlier 60 of them also run on 32-bit ARM under qemu; a mutation pass over the glue: 29 of 30 caught, the survivor is equivalent (`net.md`, `tests/net_gvex_test.c`).

**S2: they hurt.** The server applies a ghoul's attack to the player's health (the same `DamagePlayer` rules as single player), the owner's state carries health, and death does something. The death rule is decided (below); the health model (regenerating health plus armour, or medication only) is not yet.

**S3a: fists (built, round 10).** A player in a session starts with their hands only (`Arsenal_Init`), kept per player on the server (`NetServerClient.arsenal`). The fire bit works that arsenal every tick (`RunHands` in `net_server.c`: the same windup, blow and cooldown as the single-player game, hands: 10 damage, a blow every 0.45 s, the first 0.12 s after the press). When a blow connects it is judged by `NetServer_MeleeTargets`: against each creature **where the puncher's screen showed it** (the new `NetMonHistory`, a ring of every creature's position per tick, rewound by the command's `viewLag`, held to `NET_REWIND_MAX`), by the rule the solo game uses (`EnemyMelee_Reaches` in `enemy_plan.h`: reach to the creature's surface, the arc, the height; solo's `EnemiesMeleeHit` now calls the same function). Each creature hit takes the weapon's damage (`GvexWorld_Damage`: a kill removes it) and is knocked back along the line from the puncher (`GvexWorld_Knock`, which slides along walls). Nobody has to be told: the creature's health byte is already in every snapshot and a killed creature stops being listed, so the hit and the kill are visible to everyone; the screens flash a creature red for about a sixth of a second when its health drops (`play_ui.c`, drawing only). No protocol change. The server program loads the weapon numbers baked into the build (`GameConfig_LoadBaked`), so it needs no file. **Not in S3a (next small slices):** nothing is drawn of the puncher's own hands in a session (the single-player viewmodel is not wired to a session screen) and other players' punches are not drawn on their bodies; no wear (the hands never wear); the hands are all a player can hold until S3b.

**S3: shots hurt them.** The fire bit makes the server fire from an `Arsenal` kept per player (`sim.md`, "N players in `GameSim`"), the shot is judged against the creatures' rewound positions (the creature history is the same ring as `NetHistory`, for creatures), health drops, hit and kill events go to everyone, ammo goes in the owner's state, and the shooter's own screen shows the flash at once. This is steps 2 and 3 of `net_shots.md` done for creatures first.

**S4: the second and third creatures.** The Aracnid (the web as a slow and a snare, the light that blinds, the three variants) and the worm swarm with its Prime Specimen. They are where relationships earn their keep: `governs` (Prime to colony), `spawned_by` (MajourBuzzCunt to its brood), `divided_from` (Lumbricus Major). The swarm's 240 dots are not replicated one by one; what a client needs is its core, its worms' heads and its state, and the dots can be simulated from them on each screen (to be designed, and to be checked for determinism).

**S5: the typed layer.** `monsters.mdix` becomes the registry (designations, tiers, senses, numbers) and generates what S1 to S4 wrote by hand (`net_shots.md` step 5).

Single-player keeps its own enemy modules until the Flecs ones reach parity, then the old ones go; two implementations of the same creature are the thing to avoid for long.

## Decisions (mine, to be confirmed or changed)

1. **Vendor Flecs or fetch it.** Done as recommended: the pinned amalgamation is in `third_party/flecs/` (5.2 MB of source in the repository), because the work happens on a phone over GitHub and a build that needs the network for a dependency is the build that fails at the wrong moment. Say if you would rather have it fetched.
2. **Death in a session: decided by the owner (2026-11-27).** A player at 0 health is **downed**, and a teammate can heal them back up. A player killed while downed becomes **audience only**, spectating their team (`design/06-multiplayer.md`, "The audience"). Not built yet: it needs the downed state, a bleed-out timer (a placeholder to tune), the heal action and the spectator view, in S2.
2b. **Health in a session: decided by the owner (2026-11-28).** Two layers. The **health** regenerates, slowly, after a quiet spell, and comes back to **full**. The **extra health** (armour-like) never regenerates and is only restored by something like a medkit; **damage takes the extra health first**, then the health. **HUD:** the regular health never shows on the HUD; the extra health bar shows only while there is some. Not built: it comes with S2 (the delay, the speed and the cap are placeholders to tune in a `.mdix` file; the HUD's health bar behind `HUD_VITALS_ALWAYS_ON` and the health row of the stats popup are to be settled with it).
3. **Hit testing for S3** (`net_shots.md`, decision 4): hitscan against rewound poses first, projectiles judged for their whole flight later. Recommended because it is the smaller step and the shot seed already makes every pellet reproducible.
4. **Teams** (players hurt each other but not teammates, as you decided) are a relationship too; they are not needed for S1 to S3 if a session starts with one team.

## Flecs in the repository

`third_party/flecs/` holds `flecs.c` and `flecs.h` unchanged from the Flecs 4.2.0 distribution (commit `7d1bc7d`, 2026-11-24, MIT licence kept), a wrapper `tob_flecs.h` that defines the custom build (`FLECS_CUSTOM_BUILD` and only `FLECS_SYSTEM`, `FLECS_PIPELINE`, `FLECS_TIMER`, `FLECS_MODULE`, `FLECS_PARSER`, `FLECS_QUERY_DSL`, `FLECS_LOG`, `FLECS_OS_API_IMPL`) and **must be included instead of `flecs.h`** (the same macros have to be defined where `flecs.c` is compiled and where `flecs.h` is read), and `tob_flecs.c`, the one translation unit that compiles it (`-std=gnu99`; about 10 seconds, a 1.4 MB object on x86; 0.5 MB of code on armv7). `build_server.sh` compiles it on its own; `build_android.sh` compiles it as a separate unit when the driver library is in (`-DTOWER_GVEX=1`), with `-w` for Flecs's own warnings; the web build does not have it. `third_party/flecs/README.md` says how to refresh it.

## `gvex_world.h` and `gvex_world.c`

**What it does:** one Flecs world per `GvexWorld`, no file-scope state (two worlds in one process do not touch: tested), compiled only with `TOWER_GVEX` (`platform.h`: native only, an `#error` on the web; otherwise an empty unit). `GvexWorld_Create(map, tier)` spawns the floor's ghouls from `EnemyPlan_Spawns`; `GvexWorld_Step(w, targets, n, dt)` runs the pipeline once with the players as `GvexTarget`s (a position and the noise they make); `GvexWorld_Records` reads the living out in id order; `GvexWorld_Damage` hurts or kills one; `Gvex_Designation` and `Gvex_Name` give the dossier's words for a kind.
**Decisions:** components are plain data and are defined from C by name and size (`ecs_component`), the ids kept in the world so that two worlds never share a global; systems are declared with a query string, which needs the query DSL addon; the targets reach the systems through the system context, not through globals; a creature's id is its spawn index, which both ends know and which seeds its looks; the world does not know about the network.

## Not built: what the weapons need

The weapon code of the solo game (`weapons.c`, `gunfire.c`, `projectiles`, `pickups.c`) keeps its state at file scope like the monsters did, and a session starts with nothing but hands (your decision), so a gun has to be **found**. The slices that follow S1 and S2: **S3a** the fists and the shot on the server (hitscan against the creatures' rewound positions, an `Arsenal` per player, ammo and cooldowns on the server, the shot made at once on the shooter's screen, tracers to the others), **S3b** weapons on the floor as replicated pickups and the pick-up, drop and swap as requests the server checks (`net_shots.md` step 5 is what makes those cheap to add). The reading of the weapon code that decides how is the next piece of work.

**The attack's edge (fixed after the first phone test, which showed the attack glitching).** The first version attacked at 1.8 m or less and chased otherwise, and a creature that is attacking stands still, so a player moving by a few centimetres across the 1.8 m line flipped it between attacking and chasing every tick, and the screens drew its crouch on and off at 30 Hz. Now the attack is held across a band: it starts at 1.8 m and ends at 1.35 times that (`GVEX_ATTACK_HOLD`); inside the band, past its reach, the creature keeps attacking and walks closer, never nearer than 98% of its reach. The screens ease the crouch, swell and warm colour in and out (about 70 ms) instead of switching them (`play_ui.c`, drawing only). Tests: the state never changes while a player wobbles across 1.8 m for 60 ticks; a player at 2.2 m who never came closer is chased; one who steps back to 2.2 m is still attacked and the creature closes to its reach; one at 2.6 m ends the attack; six deliberate breakages of the rule were each caught. The 1.35 and the 70 ms are first guesses for the feel.

## Not verified

- **Fists on the phones:** the server side is proven on a real loopback link with latency, jitter and loss, and by mutation; what a punch looks like on a screen (the red flash) and whether the numbers feel right (damage 10, a blow every 0.45 s, a push of 0.4 m) need a look on two devices. With no hands drawn on the puncher's own screen, the only sign of a punch is the creature's flash, shove and health.
- **The attack fix on the phones:** found by reading the code and tested on the server; the visual (the eased crouch) cannot be seen without a screen. Needs a look on the devices.
- **Flecs on the other targets:** compiled cleanly with `-Wall -Wextra` for 32-bit ARM (gcc), and both creature tests pass there under qemu; **not tried with the NDK's clang** (the Android workflow is the first time), nor on a phone; the cost on a phone is not measured. The web build (emscripten) does not have sessions, and single-player will need Flecs for the web once the old modules go: not tried.
- **The snapshot size estimate** and how many creatures one datagram holds.
- **The Rust driver's cost with a larger snapshot** on a phone's radio.
