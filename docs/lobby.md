# lobby

## Overview

The lobby is the first screen of the game. It shows who the player is (the chosen character), and has four buttons: **CHOOSE / CHANGE CHARACTER** (opens the character screen, `characters.md`), **PLAY SOLO** (starts the game, exactly as the game always started), **FIND GAMES** (a list of the games found on the network, with a JOIN button) and **HOST A GAME** (opens a game that others on the network can find and join). It is three screens of its own, HOME, FIND and CONNECTING, and a hosted or joined game has a fourth, the session screen (below). It is also the place the character screen is reached from: the character screen no longer opens by itself at launch.

**What is real and what is not.** Real: PLAY SOLO, the character button, the list in FIND (the LAN discovery of `net.md`: a game on the same Wi-Fi or hotspot shows up in it by itself, with its name, its players and its transport), **hosting** (HOST opens a game, announces it, and plays in it as its first player) and **joining** (JOIN connects to a found game). What hosting and joining lead to is a **session screen**, not yet a world: who is in the game, how to reach it, how the connection is doing. **Walking together is not built**: nobody sees anyone in a 3D world, and the players' commands are "stand still" (the next step: the other players drawn and the real input sent, `net.md`, "what comes next"). **None of it has been tried on a phone or between two devices yet**: it ran over this machine's loopback, with real sockets and a real searcher. The screens do not pretend: a button appears only where the thing behind it exists, and a button that does not is shown off with the reason on it (a browser's HOST says it needs the app).

**The lobby is the first screen only.** After dying or at the end of a run the game does what it did before (see `floor_run.md`); it does not come back to the lobby yet.

## What the screens look like

800 by 450, like the character screen (cards, text and buttons in canvas pixels).

**HOME.** Top left, the title (TOWER OF BABYLON, HORROR GAME SHOW). Left, a box headed YOUR CONDEMNED: the chosen character's short name large, then the full name, the role and the overview (up to six lines), or NOBODY CHOSEN YET with "Choose who climbs the tower." when nobody is; at its foot a button, CHOOSE CHARACTER, or CHANGE CHARACTER once somebody is chosen. Right, a column of three big buttons: a green **PLAY SOLO** (off, with "CHOOSE A CHARACTER FIRST" on it, until somebody is chosen); **FIND GAMES** ("ON THIS NETWORK"); **HOST A GAME** (on when the platform can host and somebody is chosen, "OTHERS ON THIS NETWORK CAN JOIN"; off with "CHOOSE A CHARACTER FIRST", or in a browser with "NEEDS THE APP, NOT A BROWSER"). Under the column, a line saying whether the player is ready (or, in red, why a host or a join did not work, or why a game ended) and a grey line saying that hosting and joining work and walking together is the next step.

**FIND.** Title FIND A GAME and a BACK button top right (in the same place as the character screen's). A list of up to five rows at a time, each a game: its name large, "1 / 4 PLAYERS" and the transport (UDP or QUIC) small, and, for a game that cannot be joined, the reason in red on the right (OTHER VERSION, NEEDS QUIC, CANNOT CONNECT, FULL). More than five games: UP and DOWN arrows on the right. Under the list a status line: SEARCHING THE NETWORK... (with moving dots), "2 GAMES FOUND", or why there is no list (below), then two grey lines (what the list is, and "Highlight a game, then press JOIN") and, on the right under the list, a green **JOIN** button (on while the highlighted game can be joined). When a join failed the status line is replaced by the reason in red (THE GAME IS FULL, NO ANSWER, OTHER VERSION OF THE GAME, ...). **CONNECTING**, after JOIN: the game's name large, WAITING FOR THE HOST... with moving dots, and a **CANCEL** button. **The session screen** (`session_ui.c`), once a game is hosted or joined: HOSTING: or JOINED: and the game's name, a LEAVE button top right, PLAYERS 2 / 4 and four rows (each player: PLAYER n, tagged HOST and/or YOU, with their character; an empty row says WAITING FOR A PLAYER...), and on the right how to reach the game (a host lists its addresses with the port, to type if the list does not show it) and the connection: ping, snapshots, corrections, the server tick.

**Taps and keys.** A tap on a button that is on presses it; a tap on an off button does nothing. On FIND a tap on a row highlights it, and JOIN joins the highlighted game (a deliberate second tap, as on the character screen: a thumb that lands wrong joins nothing); while CONNECTING only CANCEL does anything. Keyboard: A / D or the arrow keys step through the buttons that are on (HOME) or through BACK and the games (FIND), Enter or Space presses (on a game that can be joined it joins it; while connecting it cancels; on the session screen it leaves); the focused button is outlined in yellow while the keyboard is the control scheme in use, and a hint line names the keys. BACK on the character screen has no key, only a click or tap.

## Flow (`main.c`)

`AppScreen`: `SCREEN_LOBBY` (the first), `SCREEN_CHARACTER`, `SCREEN_NET` (a hosted or joined game), `SCREEN_GAME`. The lobby's CHOOSE CHARACTER opens the character screen with the highlight on the chosen character; CHOOSE (or BACK) there returns to the lobby; PLAY SOLO starts the game: the fixed-step clock and the command latch are reset and the game fades in, as after a door. While either screen is up none of the game runs (no steps, no monsters, no clock, no 3D). With no characters at all (`Characters_Count() == 0`) PLAY SOLO is on at once and the character button says NO CHARACTERS (off), as the game with no roster always started without a screen. **Hosting:** HOST (`LOBBY_WANT_HOST`) opens the game with `NetSession_Host` (a floor seed taken from the clock, the chosen character, and the name "<CALL>'S TOWER", or "TOWER" with no roster) and goes to the session screen; if the game cannot be opened (no free port, a browser) the reason shows in red on HOME. **Joining:** JOIN (`LOBBY_WANT_JOIN`) takes the target the lobby copied (address, port and transport of the game as it was when JOIN was pressed) and calls `NetSession_Join`; the lobby shows CONNECTING; each frame `NetSession_Update` runs until the game answers (the session screen opens) or does not (back to FIND with the reason: no answer after 6 seconds, full, another version, no QUIC in this build). CANCEL leaves the attempt. **Leaving:** LEAVE (or Enter) says goodbye and returns to the lobby; a game that ends by itself (the host left, the connection was lost) returns to the lobby with the reason in red. A hosting phone that leaves closes the game and tells the others.

## Modules

### `lobby_backend.h`
**What it does:** the whole of what the lobby needs from the network, as a struct of function pointers (`LobbyBackend`): can a search run here (and if not, the reason in words), start a search (it may fail), stop it, update it once per frame, how many games there are, and the nth (`LobbyEntry`: name, players, maximum, transport, joinable, and a reason when not). **Decisions:** the screen never touches a socket, so the logic is tested with a backend that answers from a script, and the real one can change underneath it. Strings are plain ASCII and are copied by the lobby, so a backend may reuse its buffers. A `LobbyEntry` also says where and how to connect: the address the answer came from (dotted), the game's port and the transport (`NET_TRANSPORT_UDP` or `_QUIC`).

### `lobby.h` and `lobby.c`
**What it does:** the logic and the layout of the screens, with no raylib in it. `Lobby_SetContext(rosterCount, hasChoice)` is given every frame; `Lobby_Move`, `Lobby_Press` and `Lobby_Tap` take the input; `Lobby_Update(backend, nowMs)` is where the search is started and stopped and the games copied in; `Lobby_TakeWant()` hands over once what the screen wants from the game (`LOBBY_WANT_CHARACTER`, `LOBBY_WANT_PLAY`): it neither opens the character screen nor starts the game itself. `Lobby_BeginConnect`, `Lobby_ConnectFailed`, `Lobby_BackHome` and `Lobby_SetNotice` are how main.c tells the screen about a host or a join it started; `LOBBY_WANT_HOST`, `LOBBY_WANT_JOIN` (with `Lobby_JoinTarget`) and `LOBBY_WANT_CANCEL` are what it asks.
**Decisions:**
- **The buttons that are on:** CHARACTER when there is a roster; PLAY when there is no roster or somebody is chosen (the same rule the game had: no roster, no screen); FIND always; HOST when the platform can host and the player has a character (or there is no roster), the same rule as PLAY. The focus is always on a button that is on: it moves when the one it was on goes off, and when somebody has just been chosen it jumps to PLAY (the next thing to do), once.
- **The backend is called in one place.** Taps and keys only change the screen; `Lobby_Update` compares "is FIND open" with "is a search running" and starts or stops the search to match. A search that cannot run (no backend, a browser) or fails to start is a state of its own (`LOBBY_SEARCH_UNAVAILABLE`, `LOBBY_SEARCH_FAILED`) that the screen shows in words; it is not retried every frame, only when FIND is opened again. Leaving FIND stops the search and forgets the games.
- **The games are a copy**, refreshed every frame while searching, so the backend's own table can change under the screen. At most 16 (`LOBBY_MAX_ENTRIES`, the discovery's table size) and the backend is never asked about more than that. A game the backend says is gone is left out; the others keep their order (the discovery table's order).
- **The highlight is an index, not an identity**: if the list shrinks the highlight moves to the last game; if the game it was on goes and another takes its place the highlight stays on the row. Nothing can be done with a highlighted game yet, so this costs nothing now. When joining exists it should follow the game (its address and port), not the row.
- **Scrolling:** the keyboard moves the highlight and the view follows it; the arrows on screen move the view and leave the highlight alone (a view that jumped back to the highlight would make the arrows useless).
- **BACK is where the character screen's BACK is**, so a thumb learns one place.
- **The layout** is functions returning `UiRect`s, in canvas pixels, so the tests check it without a window: the character box, the four buttons, the status box, BACK, the list, five rows, the two arrows.
- **JOIN is a deliberate second step.** Tapping a game highlights it; JOIN (or Enter on the highlighted game) joins it, and only a game that can be joined (`Lobby_CanJoin`: not full, a version and a transport this build speaks) can be. The target is **a copy taken when JOIN was pressed** (`joinTarget`): the list is refreshed every frame, and main.c reads the target a moment after the press, so a list that changed in between would otherwise join a different game.
- **CONNECTING is a screen of the lobby**, not of the game: it stops the search (the list is not on screen), only CANCEL does anything, and a failed join returns to FIND with the reason, which starts the search again.
- **The red line (`notice`)** is one string for everything that went wrong or ended: it stays until the player presses something, opens FIND, or goes BACK; `Lobby_BackHome` keeps it so a game that ended can say why.

### `lobby_net.h` and `lobby_net.c`
**What it does:** the real backend over `net_discovery.h`. Searching opens a `NetDiscClient` (on `NET_DISC_PORT` unless a test says otherwise, `LobbyNet_UsePort`), and each update asks when a round is due and takes the answers; the games it lists are the table of the client. `LobbyNet_EntryFromLobby` turns one into a `LobbyEntry`: joinable means a version this game speaks, a transport this build has, and room for one more; otherwise the reason, in this order: OTHER VERSION, then NEEDS QUIC (a QUIC game on a build without QUIC) or CANNOT CONNECT (a UDP game on a build without UDP), then FULL. Where there is no UDP (the browser) `canSearch` is false and the reason is "A BROWSER CANNOT SEARCH THE NETWORK. USE THE APP." **Decisions:** one static client, because one lobby needs one search; `LobbyNet_Shutdown` is safe at any time and is called when the game ends. On Android this needs the INTERNET permission, which the manifest now declares (`android.md`). Each entry carries where to connect (the address the answer came from, never what the host claims about itself), the port the host announced, and the transport.

### `lobby_ui.h` and `lobby_ui.c`
**What it does:** draws the three screens (HOME, FIND, CONNECTING) (`LobbyUI_Draw(lobby, showKeys)`), clearing the background itself. Plain `DrawText` and rectangles, like the HUD and the character screen, and the same word wrap (`CharSelect_Wrap`) for the character's overview. It reads the chosen character from the characters module and the games from the `Lobby` (never from the backend). No test can run it (it needs a window); it is compiled for web, Android and desktop by `tests/platform_check.sh`.

### `session_ui.h` and `session_ui.c`
**What it does:** draws the screen of a hosted or joined game (`SessionUI_Draw(session, showKeys)`) and gives the layout of its one button (`SessionUI_Leave()`, a header-only function so a test can check it: top right, where BACK is). The rows come from `NetSession_Slots` (`net.md`); the addresses a host shows are read from the interfaces (ranked best first, three at most, refreshed every two seconds). It is compiled for all three platforms and drawn by no test. **Decisions:** until walking together exists this screen is also the way to see that a network works at all, so it shows what a person debugging two devices needs: who is in, the address to type, the ping, how many snapshots arrived and how many corrections the client made.

### `main.c`: `LobbyFrame`, `NetFrame`, `SelectFrame`, `BeginRun`
**What it does:** `Frame()` starts with the screen switch. `LobbyFrame` polls the config (so the real `characters.mdix` can arrive), gives the lobby its context, reads the menu and the tap, updates the lobby with the clock (`GetTime() * 1000`), keeps the audio's gesture unlock going, acts on what the lobby wants, and draws. `SelectFrame` does the same for the character screen and, when the player chose, stores the id and goes back to the lobby (BACK also goes back, storing nothing). `BeginRun` is what used to be the end of the selection screen: reset the clock and the command latch, fade in. `NetFrame` runs `NetSession_Update` every frame, reads LEAVE (a tap on its button, or Enter), draws the session screen, and returns to the lobby when the player leaves or the game ends by itself (with the reason as the red line). `LobbyFrame` also runs `NetSession_Update` while the lobby is CONNECTING, and acts on the wants HOST, JOIN and CANCEL.

## Trying hosting and joining on two devices

1. Both devices on the same Wi-Fi, or one phone's hotspot with the other connected to it, and the **same build** on both (a game of another version is listed as OTHER VERSION and cannot be joined).
2. On the first device: choose a character, tap **HOST A GAME**. The session screen opens: HOSTING: <NAME>'S TOWER, PLAYERS 1 / 4 (the host is player 1, playing in its own game), and the addresses the device can be reached at, with the port.
3. On the second: choose a character, tap **FIND GAMES**. The game should be listed within a couple of seconds, with its name, "1 / 4 PLAYERS" and UDP. Highlight it and tap **JOIN**: CONNECTING, then the session screen, JOINED: with PLAYERS 2 / 4 on **both** screens, the host's row tagged HOST and your own tagged YOU. The ping, snapshots and corrections on the right should be moving (snapshots count up; corrections stay at or near 0 standing still).
4. **LEAVE** on the joiner: the host's list goes back to one player. LEAVE on the host: the joiner's session ends and the lobby shows THE HOST LEFT in red.
5. If FIND GAMES stays empty: look at the host's session screen for its addresses and port, and try them from the other device with `tower_bot ADDRESS PORT` in Termux (`bash scripts/build_server.sh`, then `build/tower_bot`). A bot that connects proves the host and the network; then only the broadcast is in question (Wi-Fi client isolation, or a hotspot that does not pass broadcasts). Send what each screen says.

## What changed for hosting and joining (a record)

**New modules** (`net.md` has each one's section): `src/net/net_host.c/.h` (a hosted game: the server, its socket and its announcer, opened, updated and closed from any loop), `src/net/net_dial.c/.h` (one outgoing connection over UDP or QUIC), `src/net/net_session.c/.h` (host or joiner as a state machine with reasons in words), `src/lobby/session_ui.c/.h` (the session screen).
**Changed:** `src/server/server_main.c` now runs the same `NetHost` loop the game does (so there is one implementation; `tests/server_smoke.sh` and the QUIC variant pass unchanged on it) and `scripts/build_server.sh` links `net_host.c` into the server only; `LobbyEntry` gained the address, port and transport to connect with; the lobby logic gained HOST (on when the platform can host and a character is chosen), JOIN, CONNECTING with CANCEL, `Lobby_JoinTarget` (a copy taken at the press), the red notice line, and the wants HOST, JOIN and CANCEL; `main.c` gained `SCREEN_NET`, `NetFrame` and the glue for the three wants, and ends a session on exit. `Lobby_SetContext` gained a fourth argument (`canHost`).
**What had to be true for it to work, and how each was made so:**
- *Something has to announce.* The list only shows games that answer the search, and a device running only the app announced nothing. HOST is what makes the device announce (`NetHost` opens the discovery answerer on the discovery port next to the game's own port).
- *The host is a player of its own game through a real connection to 127.0.0.1*, the same path as everyone else, so there is no second way to play that could differ.
- *Where to connect is the address the answer came from*, never what the host says about itself: a phone has several addresses (Wi-Fi, hotspot, mobile data), and the one that heard the answer is the one known to work from here.
- *A busy port is not a failure:* the default 7777 falls back to a free port (a second game on one machine, a `tower_server` left running); the port in use is announced, and the session screen shows it.
- *The goodbye has to leave the device before the socket closes*, so leaving waits up to 150 ms, and a closing host tells every player (a Disconnect, `THE HOST LEFT`) instead of letting them time out after 5 seconds.
- *A join that is not answered must not hang*: 6 seconds, then NO ANSWER, back to the list with the reason.
- *The list changes under the finger*, so the game to join is copied at the press.
**Tests added:** `tests/net_session_headless.c` (43 checks over real UDP), the lobby test grew from 110 to 156 checks, 34 more deliberate breakages of the code all caught. **Measured, not guessed:** `NetHost` is 598 KB, `NetDial` 578 KB, `NetSession` 1.2 MB.

## Not verified

- **Nothing here has been seen on a screen.** The drawing (`lobby_ui.c`) compiled for all three platforms and nothing more: whether the text sizes (12 px for the small lines) are comfortable on the phone, whether the two-line buttons, the red reasons and the yellow focus outline look right, and whether the five rows with the arrows are easy to hit.
- **Nothing about the network has run between two devices.** The search, hosting, joining and the session screen ran on this machine's loopback (a real searcher, real sockets, several sessions in one process). A real Wi-Fi or hotspot with two phones has not been tried: whether the phone's network lets the broadcast out and the answer in (some Android versions drop broadcasts with the screen off; Wi-Fi with client isolation stops devices seeing each other, `net.md`), whether a hosting phone can be reached at the address it shows, and cross-device behaviour. If the list stays empty, the host's session screen shows its addresses with the port: they can be tried with `tower_bot ADDRESS PORT` from another device (`net.md`).
- `LobbyFrame` and `SelectFrame` cannot run without raylib. The logic they call is tested; the glue (the order of the calls, switching screens, the fade-in, the clock reset) is not. A finger still down from a button press does not count as a new tap on the next screen (`Input_NewTap` reports a touch once), but it was not seen.
- The browser build has no search and cannot host: FIND shows why and HOST says it needs the app. Joining from a browser needs a typed address and a transport the browser has; neither is decided (`net.md`).
- The lobby comes first only; coming back to it after a run is not built.

## Fixes and Problems

- **A test expected the wrong number:** six steps down the list from the first game land on the seventh, not the sixth. The test was wrong, not the code; it now checks five steps and then one more.
- **One mutation survived the first version of the tests:** removing the clamp on the number of games the backend reports. The loop that fills the table has its own limit, so nothing the old checks looked at changed; but the clamp is what bounds the number of questions asked of a backend that reports a huge count. A check was added (a backend reporting 30 games with one gone is asked about exactly 16).
