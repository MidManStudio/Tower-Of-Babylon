# World and lore

This is your universe, organised so the game can use it. The game needs a small part of it; the rest is the novel it shares a universe with. What the game itself should carry is at the end.

## The universe of Babylon (yours, condensed)

- The universe **balances itself**. **Agents** are ancient primordial beings that drift through space; when something grows too powerful they show up, and the result is usually a planetary wipe-out or worse. They range from the size of a city to the size of a galaxy; most are about the size of a planet, "small ones, but big regardless".
- **Earth** was destroyed by a black hole, or so people believe, and most of humanity with it. Before everything was lost, **first contact** happened: the **niu galactic federation's planetary rescue response** arrived and saved who it could, bringing them to the **all world**, a huge refugee planet where people who lost their worlds live until a new planet is found. It is a melting pot of cultures and races, with racism, conspiracies and everything else you would expect, and strict federation rules that are not always enforced.
- The **niu** are an ancient alien race with only **three surviving members**, who are **immortal**; they lead the federation and nobody has seen them.
- The federation is **peacemaker of the universe** and fights against the agents. They are not simply the good guys: they care about efficiency and will sacrifice entire planets if it serves the end goal.
- **Ascension:** you can ascend, only it is almost impossible, and only the soul ascends. It is the only way to fight the agents on roughly equal terms, because conventional weapons cannot touch the laws of existence. You have to die and not die: sent to the bottom of a sea on a huge mostly-water planet, certain death, and if you somehow die and not die you gain control of your soul and can rebuild your body and gain a degree of control over the laws of the universe. Only a handful of beings have done it.
- The main character of the novel is a **human orphan** who fixed an ancient, worn-down teleportation station over years of odd jobs; when it worked it sent him to the niu headquarters, where he overheard what he should not have, was caught, and was brought before the three niu, who put him with the participants of the **ascension**.

(You noted that the game does not explore this and it is background. I have kept it as notes only.)

## The federation (yours)

The niu galactic federation: the peacemakers who fight the agents. Efficient, ruthless, and the organisation that put every character in the Tower.

## The Tower of Babylon (yours)

An endless tower full of otherworldly horrors, run as **entertainment**, like gladiators: the most condemned criminals on death row are taken by the federation and put in teams or alone with the goal of climbing, surviving, and taking out the other teams until only one team is left; that team is disbanded because only one can survive, and that survivor is granted immunity for all past crimes and freedom. It is **broadcast across the galaxy**, so there are cameras and drones in the tower.

**Locations:**
- *On the first floor:* the spawn points, small rooms where teams are spawned randomly.
- *On all floors:* the stairs, and an elevator (not always active).
- *On specific floors:* a boss on every tenth floor.
- *On random floors:* a shop, where points buy things; the safe zone around it is guarded by two sentinels.
- No two floors are the same: the tower is ever changing.

## The federation's record (yours, the dossier of 2026-11-25)

The cosmic dossier you gave adds to the notes above; nothing in it contradicts them.

- **Governance.** The federation is the dominant interstellar authority and the self-proclaimed peacekeeper of the known cosmos. Under its public image of law, planetary rescue and order it is a cold, ruthless war machine driven by strategic efficiency, not morality. It judges every civilisation, planet and lifeform by its survival probability and its resource cost: it will evacuate billions when that suits it and give up whole populated worlds without a thought if that keeps the wider balance.
- **The three immortal Niu** sit at the summit, ancient and enigmatic; almost no citizen or officer has seen them. Every galactic directive, quarantine order and secret ascension trial comes from their unseen council.
- **The Agents** drift through deep space, from the size of a metropolis to whole planets and, in extreme cases, galaxies, and exist only to rebalance existence when a civilisation, a technology or a power grows too dominant. Their arrival is almost always total planetary annihilation or worse, and conventional weapons are useless against beings that act as living laws of existence. **The federation's real reason to exist is to fight and delay the Agents**, with everything from mass refugee relocation to forbidden metaphysical experiments.
- **Humanity's fall.** Earth was destroyed by a black hole (or so the official records say), taking most of the human race. Before humanity was entirely gone the federation's Planetary Rescue Response fleets arrived and took the survivors off-world.
- **The All-World** is a colossal refugee planet, a temporary holding ground for the survivors of lost civilisations until new colony worlds are found, packed with displaced alien species: a volatile melting pot of cultures, tension, friction between species and tightly enforced federation law.
- **Soul Ascension.** Mortal weapons cannot match the Agents, so the Niu oversee a near-impossible metaphysical process. The body cannot ascend, only the soul, and it takes a lethal ordeal at the bottom of a vast oceanic world, where the subject must in effect die without truly dying. The few who succeed gain sovereign control of their soul, the ability to rebuild their body, and authority over the fundamental laws of the universe.
- **The Tower of Babylon broadcast.** The worst condemned criminals (warlords, cult mass-murderers, bio-terrorists, rogue assassins) are not executed but turned into spectacle, broadcast live across the galaxy by drones and surveillance feeds: death-row inmates are dropped into the Tower to fight G.V.E.X. horrors and rival squads, with total immunity and freedom promised to the one final survivor.

## How much of this the game should say

Suggestions:

1. **The show is the lore.** The game can say almost everything through the broadcast: the commentator introduces each contestant by crime and sentence, reads out kills, mocks failures; the character select is a "contestant file" with a mugshot, a charge sheet and odds. A player learns who these people are from how they are sold to the galaxy, which is also how the federation would present them, and it lets the in-game text be short, in a voice, and unreliable. The deeper story stays in your novel.
2. **The audience is the player after death.** See `06-multiplayer.md`: the eliminated become part of the broadcast, which makes the setting useful to the design.
3. **Four things the player must know**, in the first two floors: (1) you are a condemned criminal in a show, (2) the stairs and the collapse, (3) the other teams are enemies and, in the end, your own team is too, (4) one survivor goes free. Everything else is optional reading.
4. **The ending** ("gain your freedom", "hopefully the niu federation will keep its word... probably") is a good place for an ambiguous beat: the winner's pardon is read out, and the last shot is the federation deciding whether to honour it.

## Tone

Dark, mocking, dry. The commentator is gleeful; the federation is bureaucratic; the monsters are horrifying; the characters are monsters too. Your notes' tone toward the cast is already fine for this.

## Content and ratings (practical, not a refusal)

- The game's violence is a given (a death-row gladiator horror game). That is within what most stores allow with an appropriate age rating, and the rating questionnaires (IARC, the one that Google Play and others use) ask about it directly, so answer them honestly and set the rating accordingly. Expect a mature rating.
- The one item I would revisit is the sexual violence in one character's record (`02-characters.md`). Stores tend to treat sexual violence differently from other violence, it can complicate the rating and the store listing, and it is a lot to put on a character who is otherwise playful. Keeping the crime out of anything player-facing, or choosing a different atrocity, keeps the character's menace without that cost. It is yours to decide; I wrote the design documents without the detail.
- Death-row criminals as a cast is fine and part of the premise; one of them being a cult leader whose cult committed a massacre is a heavy theme handled in your notes as tragedy and not as glorification, and I would keep it that way.
